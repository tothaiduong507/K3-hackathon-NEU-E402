# Research brief — Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks

- Source level: `full_text_excerpt`
- Paper URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401v4

## Problem

The performance of large pre-trained language models on knowledge-intensive NLP tasks is limited due to their inability to access and manipulate knowledge effectively, leading to inferior results compared to task-specific architectures.

## Method

The paper introduces Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models that combine pre-trained parametric memory (seq2seq models) with non-parametric memory (a dense vector index of Wikipedia) accessed via a neural retriever. Two formulations of RAG are compared: one that uses the same retrieved passages for the entire sequence and another that can use different passages for each token.

## Data / experiments

The models were fine-tuned and evaluated on various knowledge-intensive NLP tasks, including open-domain question answering (QA), abstractive question answering, Jeopardy question generation, and fact verification. The experiments utilized a single Wikipedia dump as the non-parametric knowledge source, with documents split into 100-word chunks.

## Key findings

- RAG models set the state-of-the-art on three open-domain QA tasks, outperforming both parametric seq2seq models and task-specific retrieve-and-extract architectures.
- For language generation tasks, RAG models produce more specific, diverse, and factual language compared to a state-of-the-art parametric-only seq2seq baseline.
- RAG's retrieval mechanism allows for effective knowledge access and can be updated without retraining, demonstrating flexibility in adapting to new information.

## Limitations

- The paper does not specify limitations regarding the generalizability of the RAG models to other domains or tasks outside those tested.
- There is no mention of potential biases in the retrieved knowledge from Wikipedia or the implications of using such a source.
